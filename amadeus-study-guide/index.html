0 Overview & how to use

Amadeus is a GDS (Global Distribution System). Travel agents talk to it with short cryptic entries — abbreviated commands typed into a green‑screen terminal. This guide groups those entries into the natural order of a booking: find → sell → book → price → ticket → service.

🟦 The command chip

Anything shown as DAN BLR is typed exactly as‑is. Parts in yellow are values you replace, e.g. DDBLR.

🖥️ The terminal block

Dark boxes reproduce what the screen returns, so you recognise the real output (fare bases, *RB flags, TST images…).

🔎 The finder

Jump to Command finder to search all entries by keyword or category, then test yourself with the quiz.

Interview tip: examiners love the "what does this do and when would you use it" framing. For every pricing entry, be ready to say two things: which classes it uses (current vs cheapest) and whether it creates a TST. That single distinction is covered in the Pricing matrix.

Get fluent with these before anything else; every later section leans on them.

2 Time, time‑difference & Zulu

Time entries all start with DD ("Display Date/time"). They matter for connection planning and for reading schedules that are stored in Zulu (UTC) time.

EntryPurpose
DDBLRCurrent local time at a city
DD LON/BLRTime difference between two cities
DD LHR 1800/BLRWhat time it is in BLR when it's 18:00 in LHR
DDZZZ1800/LHRConvert a Zulu/UTC time to local at an airport
HEDDHelp for all the DD variants
Why "Zulu"?

Airlines store every departure/arrival in UTC, nicknamed Zulu (Z), so times are unambiguous worldwide. The agent converts to the traveller's local time. DDZZZ is literally "DD the ZZZulu time".

> DD LON/BLR LON IS 4H30 BEHIND BLR > DDZZZ1200/BLR 1200Z = 1730 LOCAL BLR

3 Availability & schedules

Two verbs dominate: SN (Schedule/Neutral — shows all RBDs whether open or not) and AN (Availability Neutral — shows only bookable RBDs).

SN — see everything

SN lists every RBD on a flight, even sold‑out ones. Great for understanding the cabin structure.

> SN27APRBLRBOM1100 27APR BLR→BOM, from 11:00, all classes

AN — see what's sellable

AN lists only available RBDs — the ones you can actually sell right now.

> AN27APRBLRBOM1100 Same query, available RBDs only

Anatomy of an availability entry

SN 20DEC BLRROM /XDXB,CDG │ │ │ └─ modifiers (transit, RBD, airline…) │ │ └────────── origin + destination city codes │ └───────────────── travel date └──────────────────── SN = all classes · AN = available only

Modifiers you can bolt on

ModifierExampleWhat it does
Specific timeSN27APRBLRBOM1100Search from a departure time
Cabin band…/KFFilter to a cabin (see next section)
Single RBD…/CYShow one specific booking class
Add transit…/XDXB,CDGForce connections via those points
Remove transit…/X-DXB,CDGExclude those connecting points
Air segments only…/ALH/MAA = air; keep flights (drop trains/buses)

TR — Traffic Rights: a TR note on a segment means traffic rules apply — that flight can only be sold in combination with an international sector. Watch for it on domestic legs of an international itinerary.

4 RBD & cabin (booking) classes

An RBD (Reservation Booking Designator) is the single letter that sets the price/cabin of a seat. When searching you can filter to a whole cabin band with a /K… modifier.

/KF
First class

The premium cabin. SN02DECBLRLON/KF

/KC
Business class

SN02DECBLRLON/KC

/KW
Premium economy — without economy

Pure premium‑eco display. SN02DECBLRLON/KW

/KY
Economy including premium economy

SN02DECBLRLON/KY

/KM
Economy — without premium economy

Plain economy only.

Memory hook — the letters climb down the cabin: First → business (C) → premium (W) → economy‑with‑premium (Y) → economy‑only (M). Also useful: …/CRBD shows a single specific booking class.

7 Pricing entries — the FX family

This is the heart of the interview. Every FX… entry is defined by two choices: which classes (keep the current RBDs, or drop to the cheapest) and whether it creates a TST (an active priced record vs an informative quote).

EntryClasses usedCreates TST?NatureWatch out
FXPCurrentYesActive priceIgnores cheaper options; may show *RB
FXXCurrentNoInformativeSame as FXP but quote only
FXBCheapestYesActive priceMay downgrade pax — use with filters
FXRCheapestNoInformativeCheapest quote only; may downgrade
FXALowest + listYes (via FXU)Priced listShows a menu; pick a line then create TST
Working an FXA list
EntryMeaning
FXT1When no *RB in the list, choose a priced option (line 1)
FXU1Book/select line 1 → creates the TST
FXYShow more fare combinations / upsell from the offered fare
MPFXAMove to Previous entry (MPFXA, MPFXB…)
TTE/ALLDelete all TSTs
FQN1*PEFare rules after pricing (transaction 1, penalties)
The *RB flag

In a price list, *RB marks lines that cannot be actively priced right now (no seats in that fare). If your list has no *RB, you can safely pick a line with FXT1.

FXB / FXR danger: "cheapest" can silently move the passenger to a lower cabin. Always pair them with filters (cabin, flexibility) — the examiner will want to hear this caution.

9 Pricing filters & fare conditions

Filters refine any pricing entry so you don't accidentally downgrade or overpay. Append them after the FX command.

Cabin & baggage
FilterEffect
/KCForce a cabin (here Business)
/K-Keep current cabins — price two different RBDs together
/SBFInclude baggage in fare (worldwide except the US)
/SBF works poorly in the US — better to let the pax add bags on the website or at the airport.
Flexibility conditions R,UP,U001111,*…
SuffixSelects fares that are…
*NPEFull‑flex (incl. changes/cancellations, even no‑show)
*RFRefundable
*RBChangeable (rebookable)
*NRNo restriction

Expand full fare conditions after pricing with FQN1*PE.

Careful: full‑flex (*NPE) fares also cover changes and cancellations with no‑show — flexible but pricier. Match the filter to what the traveller actually needs.

FQP — partial / manual fare quote

Build a fare "by hand" segment‑by‑segment for a partial flow ticket:

FQPJFK/AQR/CR/D20DECBOM- /AQR/CR/D20DECJFK-/R,UP,20SEP23

Pattern: FQP DEP /A airline /C class /D date ARRIVAL — repeat per leg — then ,R,UP,DOI (date of issue).

11 Round‑the‑world (RTW) fares

Special multi‑continent fares priced as one journey. They validate against alliance rules and use a dedicated pricing modifier.

EntryMeaning
FXA…/S2RWPrice the itinerary as Round‑the‑World from segment 2
FQDORIGIN-ORIGINRTW fare display — origin = destination
FQDBKKBKK/D03MAR24/R,UP,07NOV23/CD/ABAWorks with all airlines from the alliance
Rule: for RTW, origin and destination cities are the same (you circle the globe and come home), which is why the fare display uses FQDBKKBKK.

12 ATC — Automated Ticket Changes (reissue)

ATC reprices a changed itinerary and works out the fare difference + penalty. The four entries mirror the booking‑side FX family — same "current vs cheapest" and "creates elements vs informative" logic.

EntryClassesCreates exchange elements? (TST/FO/FP)Booking‑side twin
FXQCurrentYes — full exchange≈ FXP
FXOCheapestYes — may downgrade, use filters≈ FXB
FXFCurrentNo — informative, confirm with FXQ≈ FXX
FXECheapestNo — informative, confirm with FXO≈ FXR

The ATC workflow

1
Verify ticket

Ticket is issued and can no longer be voided

2
Rebook

Book the new flights

3
Price change

FXQ/FXO/FXF/FXE

4
Confirm

If you used FXF/FXE, confirm with FXQ/FXO

5
Decide

Pax agrees → document & issue. Pax declines → IR (ignore)

Reissue & repricing entries
EntryMeaning
FXF/S2-5/TKTnumReprice segments 2‑5 against an existing ticket number
FQRSee the old TST — prices only, without flight info
2/HKChange itinerary status (segment 2 → confirmed)
TTP/Tn/RTReissue the ticket from TST n
IRIgnore/undo the changes
Key idea for the interview

ATC is just "pricing, but for a change". If you can explain the FX matrix, you already understand ATC — just add "and it also calculates the fare difference + penalty and builds the exchange elements (FO, FP)."

FO = Original issue / form‑of‑ticket detail; FP = Form of payment. ATC creates these automatically for the exchange.
